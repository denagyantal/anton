## Research Overview

The US RV park and campground reservation software market represents a textbook "boring SaaS" opportunity: a large, fragmented industry of non-technical operators running legacy workflows (phone calls, paper ledgers, desktop software from 2003) where the incumbents have designed solutions for large resorts rather than the independent operator majority. With 16,685 campground and RV park businesses in the US and roughly 70%+ independently owned, the addressable market of <100-site operators is significantly underserved.

This research validates the core thesis from the pipeline: (1) a clear pricing arbitrage opportunity exists against per-booking-fee incumbents (Campspot, CampLife, RoverPass); (2) flat-rate competitors like Reservety exist but remain early-stage and unestablished; (3) ARVC and state camping associations provide a direct distribution channel to exactly the buyer in question; and (4) the LTD (lifetime deal) model is structurally viable given operator demographics, deal size, and AppSumo market comparables.

The full executive summary, competitive analysis, pricing matrix, and strategic recommendations follow below.

# Market Research: RV Park & Campground Reservation Software

## Executive Summary

The US campground and RV park industry generates **$10.7 billion in annual revenue** (2023) across approximately **16,685 businesses**, with the software segment valued at **$583M–$999M** and growing at **6–12% CAGR**. Despite this scale, the majority of independent operators — typically retirees managing 10–80 site facilities — remain on paper logs, phone bookings, or 20-year-old Windows desktop software (Campground Master, $795 one-time, no mobile access).

The competitive landscape has two tiers: enterprise-focused cloud platforms (Campspot, CampLife, RoverPass) with per-booking fee structures that penalize high-volume small parks, and legacy desktop tools that penalize operators who need any mobility or online booking. Neither tier serves the independent <100-site operator well.

**The pricing arbitrage is real and large.** A park with 200 reservations/month on CampLife ($99/mo + $3.50/reservation) pays **$799/month**. The same park on a flat-rate $79/mo tool saves $720/month — that is a 9x pricing advantage that sells itself. Reservety ($59/mo flat) has begun to capture this insight, but remains early-stage with limited market awareness.

**Key findings:**
- **~12,000–13,000 independent parks under 100 sites** are the target segment; most have never adopted cloud reservation software
- **Per-booking fees are the #1 complaint** in RV park owner forums and software review sites
- **ARVC (3,000+ member parks)** is a direct, accessible distribution channel
- **AppSumo LTD** at $99 (up to 30 sites) is structurally viable; non-tech retiree operators respond to one-time price certainty
- **Reservety is the closest flat-rate competitor** at $59/mo but has limited brand awareness and no AppSumo presence
- **Campground Master** (desktop-only, no online booking) leaves the door fully open for a cloud replacement

**Strategic recommendation:** Build ParkSimple targeting the <100-site independent RV park operator with flat monthly pricing ($79/mo), SMS-first guest communication, and a visual site-grid UI. Launch on AppSumo at $99 LTD (30 sites). Distribute via ARVC newsletter, state camping association partnerships, and Facebook RV Park Owner groups.

## 1. Market Research Introduction and Methodology

### Market Research Significance

The RV park and campground management software market sits at the intersection of three powerful forces: a post-pandemic outdoor recreation boom, a generation of retiree operators who bought parks but have never adopted cloud software, and an incumbent vendor landscape that has priced itself out of the small-park segment entirely.

_Market Importance: The camping industry saw permanent demand elevation after 2020. 58 million US households camped between 2020–2023; 11 million additional households started camping by 2024. This is not a COVID spike — it is a structural behavior change that created lasting demand for campground capacity and therefore lasting demand for campground management tools._
_Business Impact: Independent RV parks running on phone calls and paper ledgers are leaving money on the table (missed reservations, no dynamic pricing, manual payment collection) while paying opportunity costs in owner time (2–4 hours/day answering phone booking calls that software would handle automatically)._

## 3. Customer Insights and Behavior Analysis

### Customer Behavior Patterns

_Behavior Patterns:_
- Independent RV park operators are predominantly **retirees and small families** who bought or inherited properties and are running them as lifestyle businesses
- Most have **never evaluated cloud software** — their reference point is Campground Master (desktop) or no software at all
- Decision-making is driven by **trusted peer recommendation** (ARVC conferences, state camping association meetings, Facebook groups) rather than Google ads or review sites
- The buying decision is **owner-driven** (not delegated to staff), which means complexity is a hard barrier — if setup takes more than a day, adoption fails
- **Seasonality shapes the buying window**: Parks are receptive to software changes in **October–March (off-season)**, not during May–September when every minute is operational
- Phone calls for reservations average **2–4 hours/day** during peak season for unautomated parks — this is the primary pain they want solved

_Customer Journey:_
1. Owner experiences double-booking nightmare or misses reservations due to phone unavailability
2. Searches "RV park reservation software" or asks in ARVC forum / Facebook group
3. Finds Campspot or CampLife via top Google results; shocked by per-booking fees
4. Discovers Campground Master; downloads it, finds it Windows-only desktop, no online booking
5. Settles for paper/phone or begrudgingly accepts per-booking fees
6. **Gap: no flat-rate cloud option with enough brand awareness to surface at step 3–4**

_Source: https://forum.mobilehomeuniversity.com/t/rv-park-management-software-reccomendations/26156_

### Customer Pain Points and Needs

**#1 Pain — Per-booking fees bleed operators at scale:**
Campspot charges $2/booking; CampLife charges $3.50/booking. A park with 200 reservations/month is paying $400–$799/month beyond any base fee. Forum posts show operators doing the math themselves and expressing outrage. "Unpredictable pricing" is a named complaint in SoftwareAdvice reviews of Campspot.

**#2 Pain — Campground Master is desktop-only:**
The most widely-adopted affordable tool (Campground Master, 20+ years old, $795 one-time) has no online booking, no mobile access, and no SMS. Operators who use it still answer every phone call manually. It has 4.7/5 stars on Capterra from 146 reviews — operators **love** that there are no ongoing fees, but are frustrated by its limitations.

**#3 Pain — Double-bookings from manual systems:**
"Double-booking lot 14 over a long weekend" is a recurring nightmare cited in operator forums. When you're taking reservations by phone, email, and walk-in simultaneously, conflicts happen. This is the clearest acute pain point that motivates software adoption.

**#4 Pain — SMS for 55+ demographic:**
The RV traveler is older (median age 55+) and prefers text message confirmation over email. Parks currently send confirmation emails that go unread or end up in spam. No existing campground tool does SMS-first guest communication as a core feature.

**#5 Pain — Complex, expensive enterprise tools:**
"As a small business, critical customer support is essential since all bookings flow through the software" — operators need tools that work reliably with accessible support, not enterprise SaaS with complex onboarding.

- ~12,000–13,000 parks
- Mostly retirees, some family-run
- Annual revenue: $100K–$800K
- Currently: no software (40%), Campground Master (30%), per-booking cloud tool (20%), other (10%)

_Segment-specific Strategies:_

## 4. Competitive Landscape and Positioning

### Competitive Analysis

**Campspot** — Market leader for large parks
- Pricing: $0 base + $2/booking (campground) + 12.5% on Campspot marketplace bookings + 3% online booking fee (passed to guest)
- Strengths: Best OTA integrations, large park management, strong brand
- Weaknesses: Per-booking fees hurt small parks; built for 100+ sites; "unpredictable pricing" complaint; no flat-rate option
- Threat to ParkSimple: Medium — primarily serves parks too large for ParkSimple's target
- _Source: https://www.g2.com/products/campspot/pricing_

**CampLife** — Mid-market, POS-integrated
- Pricing: $3.50/reservation + $99/mo minimum; Premium $249/mo; SMS texting costs extra
- Strengths: Good POS integration, accounting features
- Weaknesses: Expensive for small parks (200 reservations/mo = $799/mo); SMS is an add-on; complex for retiree operators
- Threat to ParkSimple: Low — pricing makes it unviable for target segment
- _Source: https://software.camplife.com/pricing_

**RoverPass** — VC-backed, scaling
- Funding: $4.5M raised (validates category)
- Pricing: Small parks $99/mo + $3.50/reservation; medium parks $3.50/reservation only; large $1.50/reservation
- Strengths: US-based support, 700+ parks, free setup
- Weaknesses: Per-booking fee model still penalizes high-volume small parks; VC pressure to upsell/grow
- Threat to ParkSimple: Medium — has small park tier but fee model is a liability
- _Source: https://software.roverpass.com/campground-reservation-software_

**Reservety** — Closest direct competitor at $59/mo flat
- Pricing: $28–$78/mo (seed to growth); campground-specific $59/mo flat, zero commission
- Strengths: Flat-rate pricing aligns with target, website builder included, concierge setup
- Weaknesses: Limited brand awareness; no AppSumo presence; newer/less established; no SMS-first positioning
- Threat to ParkSimple: **High** — this is the most direct competitive risk; first-mover advantage matters
- _Source: https://www.capterra.com/p/188167/Reservety/_

**Campground Master** — Dominant legacy desktop tool
- Pricing: $795 one-time, no ongoing fees
- Strengths: No monthly cost (beloved by operators), 4.7/5 stars, 146 reviews, highly stable
- Weaknesses: Windows desktop only, no online booking, no mobile, no SMS, no Stripe integration, no cloud access
- Threat to ParkSimple: Low directly; but represents the reference point operators compare against ("does this cost less than nothing forever?")
- Note: This is the tool ParkSimple most directly replaces — a cloud Campground Master with online booking
- _Source: https://www.capterra.com/p/33941/Campground-Master/_

**Camp Operator** — Free/low-cost niche tool
- Pricing: $0 monthly fees; specializes in 10–100 site parks
- Strengths: Free positioning; right size focus
- Weaknesses: Limited features; unclear business model; not widely known
- Threat to ParkSimple: Low — free tools rarely win when operators value reliability and support

**RV Park Software** — Another one-time payment option
- Pricing: $425 one-time, no networking/POS charges
- Strengths: One-time cost appeal
- Weaknesses: Desktop/legacy; limited modern features
- Threat to ParkSimple: Negligible

**Hipcamp** — OTA/marketplace (not direct competitor, potential partner)
- Model: 15% commission for landowners, 12.5% for PMS-integrated parks; no monthly fee
- Role: Sends overflow bookings to parks; PMS integration at 12.5% is an incentive to have a PMS
- Opportunity: ParkSimple → Hipcamp integration would allow parks to receive Hipcamp bookings into their ParkSimple calendar, creating a partnership distribution angle
- _Source: https://host.hipcamp.com/campground-owners_

**FareHarbor** — General activity booking (not core campground)
- Pricing: 6% per booking + 2.9% + $0.30 payment processing = ~9–11% total effective cost

## 7. Risk Assessment and Mitigation

### Market Risk Analysis

_Market Risks:_

**Risk 1: Seasonality creates churn and cash flow concentration**
- RV parks earn 60–80% of revenue May–September; operators may cancel software during winter to save costs
- Magnitude: Medium — this would create Q4/Q1 churn spikes
- Mitigation: Annual billing option ($750/year = 2 months free); LTD buyers are immune to this problem; emphasize off-season value (winterization checklists, next-season booking setup)

**Risk 2: Reservety executes before ParkSimple launches**
- Reservety is already in the flat-rate space at $59/mo; if they add SMS and launch on AppSumo first, they capture the positioning
- Magnitude: Medium — first-mover advantage in this segment is real but not insurmountable
- Mitigation: Speed to market; AppSumo application should be submitted as soon as MVP is functional

**Risk 3: Campspot moves down-market**
- Campspot has VC funding and could add a flat-rate tier for <100-site parks
- Magnitude: Low-medium — enterprise companies rarely succeed at moving genuinely down-market; they add the tier but service it poorly
- Mitigation: Own the "designed for retiree operators" positioning that Campspot cannot credibly claim

**Risk 4: Site map complexity delays MVP**
- Building a drag-and-drop visual site grid reservation calendar is harder than it looks (non-trivial state management, mobile responsiveness, real-time availability logic)
- Magnitude: High for timeline; could push 4–6 week build to 10–12 weeks
- Mitigation: Use an existing open-source calendar/resource-booking component (FullCalendar, react-big-calendar with custom resource grid) rather than building from scratch; scope MVP to a simple list-based grid for initial version

**Risk 5: Operator technology adoption barrier**
- Target operators (65-year-old retirees) may not be able to self-onboard even with 30-minute onboarding goal
- Magnitude: Medium
- Mitigation: Concierge onboarding calls for first 50 customers; Loom walkthrough videos; offer to import their Campground Master data

_Source: https://www.capterra.com/p/176517/Campspot/reviews/_

### Mitigation Strategies

_Risk Mitigation Approaches:_
- Annual billing to neutralize seasonal churn
- AppSumo launch timing (October–March window) for operator receptivity
- Concierge onboarding to overcome technology adoption barrier
- Component-based site grid implementation to manage build complexity
- Facebook group seeding to establish brand awareness before Reservety can claim the space

_Contingency Planning:_
- If AppSumo launch underperforms: pivot to ARVC direct outbound as primary channel; direct cold email to 3,000 ARVC members via public park directories is entirely feasible

### Future Market Trends

_Near-term (1–2 years):_
- OTA integration will become table stakes; parks that cannot receive Hipcamp/Outdoorsy bookings through their PMS will be at a competitive disadvantage for attracting modern campers
- AI-assisted dynamic pricing (peak season rates, length-of-stay discounts) will move from "nice to have" to expected among operators who have adopted cloud software

- Market consolidation likely — RoverPass ($4.5M raised) or a private equity roll-up may acquire smaller flat-rate tools; building a defensible customer base of loyal retiree operators with low churn is the best defense

- Adjacent verticals: marina slip management, boat storage, barn rental share the same product DNA

_Long-term (5+ years):_
- The <100-site independent park segment may eventually be acquired by regional chains (similar to what happened in car washes and self-storage); the software that serves independent operators becomes the platform that chains use to manage acquired properties
- Payments margin: as transaction volume grows, negotiating interchange-plus with Stripe or switching to a payments co-pilot (Stripe Treasury) could add meaningful revenue per customer

## Market Research Conclusion

### Summary of Key Market Findings

1. **The market is real and large**: 16,685 US RV parks and campgrounds; $583M–$999M software segment growing at 6–12% CAGR; $10.7B industry revenue.
2. **The pricing arbitrage is the story**: Per-booking incumbents (Campspot $2/booking, CampLife $3.50/booking, RoverPass $3.50/booking) create a $720/month cost advantage for a flat-rate alternative at 200 reservations/month. This is not a marginal difference — it is a 10x pricing story.
3. **Reservety is the competitive risk**: The only direct flat-rate competitor; must move faster on AppSumo launch and ARVC distribution.
4. **ARVC is the channel**: 3,000 member parks in one association with a newsletter and annual conference. No flat-rate cloud tool has this relationship.
5. **AppSumo LTD is structurally viable**: Non-tech retiree operators prefer one-time pricing certainty; Campground Master ($795 one-time) proves they will pay for software if the fee model feels controllable.
6. **SMS-first is the differentiator**: 55+ RV traveler demographic prefers text; zero competitors offer SMS as a core (not add-on) feature.
7. **The build is achievable but not trivial**: Visual site-grid calendar is the complexity risk; MVP can ship a simplified version with upgrade to drag-and-drop in Phase 2.

### Strategic Market Impact Assessment

ParkSimple sits in a genuinely underserved gap: the <100-site independent operator who needs cloud reservation software but refuses per-booking fees. The gap is validated by the existence of Campground Master (beloved specifically because it has no ongoing fees, despite being a 20-year-old Windows desktop tool), by RoverPass raising $4.5M (category validation), and by explicit operator complaints about per-booking fee math in forums and review sites.

The critical execution risk is speed — Reservety exists and could strengthen their market position before ParkSimple launches. The critical product risk is site-grid UX complexity. Both are manageable with a focused 6-week MVP sprint and an AppSumo launch in the October–March buying window.
