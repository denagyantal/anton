## Research Overview

This report examines the market for real-time commission tracking software targeting door-to-door (D2D) sales teams in home services verticals — primarily pest control, solar installation, home security, and roofing. The research validates DASHP's $100K ARR proof point, quantifies the addressable workforce, maps the competitive landscape, and assesses go-to-market strategy including LTD viability.

The core finding: the problem is real, well-documented, and growing. An estimated 250,000–400,000 D2D sales reps across the four primary verticals work entirely on commission with no real-time pay visibility. Existing solutions either (a) do commission tracking as a side feature of a CRM/canvassing platform at $39–75/rep/mo, or (b) are payroll-integrated enterprise platforms ($35+/user/mo) that exceed the operational complexity of small D2D operators. The gap is a focused, affordable, multi-vertical commission-tracking tool built specifically for D2D operators with 5–100 reps. See Section 4 for competitive positioning and Section 5 for go-to-market recommendations.

## 1. Market Research Introduction and Methodology

The door-to-door sales industry is experiencing a technology upgrade cycle. While CRMs, territory mapping, and digital contracts have been adopted broadly, commission calculation and pay transparency remain overwhelmingly manual — managed via Excel spreadsheets that require a manager to spend Sunday nights producing the Monday paycheck. For sales reps whose entire livelihood depends on commission accuracy, this creates a trust crisis that directly drives turnover.

DASHP's $100K ARR milestone (achieved bootstrapped, solo, in pest control alone) is the clearest possible market validation signal: the product was built, buyers showed up immediately ("what is your company called and how much does it cost?" — first meeting quote), and the software now recurs. ## 3. Customer Insights and Behavior Analysis

### 3.1 Customer Behavior Patterns

**Who buys:** The primary buyer is the sales manager or company owner of a D2D operation with 5–100 reps. At the smallest scale (5–15 reps), this is often the founder themselves doing payroll manually. At 20+ reps, it's a dedicated sales ops or admin person.

**Purchase trigger:** The decision to buy commission software almost always follows a painful event: a rep dispute that blew up publicly, a payroll error that caused reps to threaten to quit, or a company hitting scale where Sunday-night spreadsheets took 4+ hours. Pain-driven purchasing = short sales cycles.

**The DASHP "first meeting" signal:** "What is your company called and how much does it cost?" as a first-meeting question (documented in Starter Story interview) is the textbook PMF signal for a painkiller product. The buyer already knows they need it; they just needed to find the product.
_Source: [I Built A $100K ARR SaaS For Pest Control Companies — Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/dashp)_

### 3.2 Demographic Segmentation

**Segment 1 — Mid-Size Pest Control Operator (5–50 reps):**
- Owner/manager age: 30–50; often former rep or college-sales veteran
- Tech comfort: Medium; uses QuickBooks, basic CRM
- Seasonality: March–September peak; needs a solution that handles seasonal hire/fire cleanly
- Budget: $500–$2,000/mo for software that eliminates payroll headaches
- Motivation: Eliminating disputes, reducing manager weekend time, retaining top reps

**Segment 2 — Solar Company Sales Director (20–200 reps):**
- Typically more tech-forward than pest control; familiar with Salesforce/HubSpot
- Commission structures are complex: per-watt, milestone triggers, team lead overrides
- Churn: High if software doesn't handle split commissions and milestone-based pay accurately
- Budget: $2,000–$10,000/mo; software is justified by a single prevented rep dispute at this scale

**Segment 3 — Roofing Company Owner (3–30 reps):**
- Least tech-sophisticated of the verticals
- Seasonal spikes around storm events; roster changes rapidly
- Strong word-of-mouth buying behavior within local roofing communities
- Budget: Conservative; values simplicity over features

**Segment 4 — Home Security Dealer Principal (10–100 reps):**
- Dealer networks (ADT, Vivint) often share software recommendations
- High rep turnover by design (churn-and-burn model common in alarm sales)
- Needs: Per-rep tracking, account install confirmation triggers, clawback for cancellations within funding window

### 3.3 Psychographic Profiles

**Values:** Fairness, transparency, avoiding drama. Every commission dispute is a relationship-damaging event. Buyers don't want software — they want to stop having Sunday-night stress and Monday-morning arguments.

**Lifestyle:** Field-based operators who live in Excel because that's all they've known. They're not resistant to software; they simply never found one that handled their specific commission structures (tiered rates, clawbacks, team lead overrides).

**Attitudes toward existing tools:** Positive toward DASHP and Sequifi (validated in G2/Capterra reviews) but skeptical of large CRM-bundled solutions because "commission tracking is always an afterthought in those products."

### 3.4 Behavior Drivers

**Emotional Drivers:**
- Fear of losing top reps over paycheck disputes (a rep who knocks 10 doors a day in Florida heat and doesn't trust their paycheck will leave)
- Pride in running a professional operation (spreadsheets feel embarrassing as the team scales)
- Anxiety reduction: real-time pay visibility for both manager and rep

**Rational Drivers:**
- Rep retention ROI: replacing one rep costs $115K–$150K; software at $1,000–$2,000/mo pays for itself if it prevents a single departure
- Manager time savings: 4–8 hours/week of spreadsheet work eliminated
- Dispute reduction: 15–25 disputes per 100 statements on spreadsheets vs 4–8 with proper software

**Social Influences:**
- Strong peer-to-peer referral networks within each vertical
- Pest control operators talk at PestWorld, NPMA; solar at SolarCon; roofing at NRCA
- Success stories spread fast: "we haven't had a commission dispute in 6 months" is a viral message

## 4. Customer Pain Points and Needs

### 4.1 The Commission Anxiety Problem

The foundational pain is **pay anxiety** — a term DASHP's founder used explicitly in describing why reps need real-time visibility. When a sales rep works commission-only, knocking 50 doors a day with no feedback loop until the Friday paycheck, their willingness to continue working (and recruiting friends) degrades measurably.

Quantified impact:
- **60%+ of reps** have experienced commission errors in the past 12 months
- **22% of reps** have at least one formal commission dispute per year
- **9% of reps** eventually quit specifically over commission errors or disputes
- Spreadsheet-based programs generate **15–25 disputes per 100 commission statements**; well-managed software programs generate **4–8 per 100**
_Source: [Losing Top Sales Reps Over Commission Issues](https://blog.salescookie.com/2026/05/04/losing-top-sales-reps-commission-issues-ai/), [Sales Commission Disputes: Complete Anatomy](https://blog.salescookie.com/2026/05/15/sales-commission-disputes-anatomy-cut-in-half/), [9% of reps quit — QuotaPath](https://www.quotapath.com/blog/poor-compensation-management/)_

### 4.2 The Clawback Calculation Problem

Clawback rules are the single most complex commission calculation in every D2D vertical:
- **Pest control**: Customer cancels within 30/60/90 days → rep owes back some or all commission
- **Solar**: Job dropped after signature but before install → milestone-based clawback calculation
- **Home security**: Account canceled within funding window → full commission reversal
- **Roofing**: Insurance claim denied post-close → job doesn't proceed

Manual clawback tracking across 50+ reps with rolling cancellation windows is "a payroll failure waiting to happen." This is the #1 source of errors and disputes in spreadsheet-managed operations.
_Source: [Best Payroll Software for D2D Sales Teams](https://blog.sequifi.com/what-is-the-best-payroll-software-for-d2d-sales-teams/), [Door-to-Door Sales Payroll & Commission Software — Sequifi](https://sequifi.com/door-to-door-commission-software)_

| Automatic clawback calculation | ✅ Critical | ✅ Critical | ⚠️ Important | ✅ Critical |

| Team lead override commissions | ⚠️ Sometimes | ✅ Important | ⚠️ Sometimes | ✅ Important |
| 1099 vs W2 in same system | ⚠️ Seasonal | ✅ Critical | ⚠️ Seasonal | ⚠️ Common |

| Leaderboard / gamification | ⚠️ Nice to have | ✅ Important | ⚠️ Nice to have | ✅ Important |

### 4.4 Adoption Barriers

**Price**: Not a real barrier — the cost of a single commission dispute exceeding a monthly software bill is universally understood by operators.

**Complexity**: The biggest barrier is onboarding. If it takes more than 30 minutes to configure a commission structure, operators will revert to Excel. Simplicity is table stakes.

**Trust**: Operators who've been burned by software that "couldn't handle our commission structure" are cautious. A free trial with real data is the only proof they'll accept.

**Seasonality**: Pest control and roofing operators hire and fire reps seasonally — billing models that charge per-seat make them anxious about onboarding 30 summer reps to a monthly contract. Billing should flex with roster size.

### 4.5 Customer Satisfaction Gaps with Current Solutions

- **Spreadsheets**: Universal frustration but default because "nothing else works for our structure"
- **CRM-bundled commission tracking**: Seen as an afterthought; typically handles only simple flat-rate commission; falls apart on clawbacks and splits
- **Enterprise solutions (Xactly, SAP)**: Vastly overbuilt and overpriced for D2D operators with 5–50 reps
- **DASHP**: Strong PMF in pest control but limited to that vertical; no solar/roofing commission templates

## 5. Customer Decision Processes and Journey

### 5.1 Decision-Making Process

The typical D2D operator commission software purchase follows this path:

1. **Crisis trigger** — A major commission dispute or 3+ hours spent on a single payroll cycle
2. **Search** — Google "pest control commission software" or "D2D commission tracking app" (low-competition keywords, confirmed)
3. **Peer check** — Post in pest control or solar Facebook group: "anyone use software for tracking commissions?"
4. **Demo / free trial** — Will test 1–2 solutions side by side with real data
5. **Purchase decision** — Happens within 1–2 weeks of first contact if the trial works; the buyer rarely shops for months

Timeline: 1–4 weeks from discovery to purchase for operators under 50 reps. Larger operators (100+ reps) may involve a 60–90 day evaluation.

### 5.2 Key Decision Criteria (in priority order)

1. **"Can it handle our commission structure?"** — The first question every buyer asks. If the demo can't input their exact commission formula in 5 minutes, they won't buy.
2. **Price** — Simple per-rep pricing preferred; concern about seasonal billing spikes
3. **Rep-facing dashboard** — Can reps see their own earnings without calling the manager?
4. **Payroll integration** — QuickBooks / Gusto export is table stakes for operators already using those platforms
5. **Clawback automation** — "How does it handle cancellations?" is asked early

### 5.3 Touchpoints and Information Sources

- **Facebook Groups**: "Pest Control Business Owners", "Solar Sales Professionals", "D2D/B2B Sales Community" are primary discovery channels
- **Reddit**: r/PestControl, r/solar, r/d2dsales — operators actively post software questions
- **Google search**: Low-competition keywords make organic content a viable customer acquisition channel
- **Word of mouth / referrals**: DASHP's entire customer acquisition strategy; no performance marketing used
- **Starter Story / Indie Hackers**: DASHP's case study created inbound; same channel available to a new entrant

### 5.4 Social Proof Requirements

Operators buying software for the first time need:
- A founder who is reachable (DASHP's founder explicitly markets personal availability)
- At least 1–2 testimonials from the same vertical ("works for pest control")
- A trial that can process real commission data before payment

### 6.4 Sequifi — The Over-Built Competitor

Sequifi is the most feature-complete solution but also the most complex and expensive:

**Sequifi's weakness for small operators**: Combining payroll and commission in one system is powerful but creates implementation friction for operators who already have a payroll provider. A 15-rep pest control company with an accountant who handles QuickBooks doesn't want to migrate their entire payroll — they just want their commission calculations automated.

D2DHQ is the most purpose-built commission OS for complex D2D commission structures:
- Flat deal-based pricing ($149 Starter / $299 Pro / $499 Elite per month) — no per-rep fees
- Deep solar support: per-watt, milestone triggers, setter/closer splits, QuickBooks sync
- Deep roofing support: insurance-contingent commission structures
- $500 one-time white-glove onboarding fee

**D2DHQ's weakness**: $149/mo minimum makes it overkill for a 5-rep pest control team doing $250/mo in commissions. Also canvassing-focused framing may not resonate with pest control operators who use different sales workflows.
_Source: [D2DHQ — Solar Sales Commission Software](https://www.d2dhq.com/industries/solar-sales-commission-software)_

### 6.6 Competitive Gap Analysis

The market has three clear unserved positions:

1. **Multi-vertical focused tool at $20–25/rep/mo** — covers pest control, solar, roofing, and home security with industry-specific commission templates, at DASHP-level simplicity but D2DHQ-level commission structure flexibility
2. **Small-team entry pricing** — $49/mo for up to 5 reps; accessible for small operators without commitment
3. **Clawback automation as first-class feature** — none of the "lighter" tools (RepCard, SalesRabbit) make clawback automation a core value proposition

### 6.7 Market Differentiation Opportunities

1. **Cross-vertical commission templates** — "pest control package" vs "solar $/watt with milestone triggers" vs "roofing insurance replacement" = the key differentiator DASHP lacks
2. **Rep-first UX** — most tools are manager-first; a mobile rep dashboard that shows "you earned $X today" is the emotional hook that drives word-of-mouth
3. **Simple onboarding** — configure commission structure in <15 minutes vs Sequifi's complex setup
4. **Flexible billing for seasonality** — DASHP's flex-license model (2× for month-to-month, capped at 30% of seats) is elegant and should be adopted

## 7. Strategic Market Recommendations

### 7.1 Market Opportunity Assessment

**Primary Opportunity**: Multi-vertical D2D commission tracking targeting the 5–100 rep market segment at $20–25/rep/mo, starting with pest control (DASHP's validated terrain) and expanding to solar and roofing within 6 months.

**Revenue potential**:
- 10 companies × 40 reps × $25/mo = **$10K MRR** (achievable milestone at 6–9 months)
- 50 companies × 40 reps × $25/mo = **$50K MRR** (12–18 month target)
- 200 companies × 50 reps × $25/mo = **$250K MRR** / $3M ARR (24–36 month target)

**Why retention is structural**: Commission tracking is payroll-adjacent. Once reps are paid via the platform and trust their dashboards, the switching cost becomes psychologically equivalent to asking reps to give up their paycheck visibility. Churn = rep revolt.

### 7.2 Beachhead vs. Expansion Strategy

**Recommended approach**: Pest control first (DASHP validates the exact buyer, pricing, and use case), then solar (largest rep workforce), then roofing.

**Reasoning**:
- Pest control operators are already aware the problem is solvable (DASHP exists)
- Pest control has the lowest commission complexity (vs. solar's milestone-based structures)
- DASHP's limitation to pest control leaves multi-vertical operators — companies that do both pest control and home security, for example — with no solution
- Solar expansion multiplies TAM 10× once pest control product is stable

### 7.3 Competitive Positioning Statement

> *"The only commission tracking tool that works for pest control today and solar tomorrow — without the payroll system complexity of Sequifi or the CRM overhead of SalesRabbit."*

**Core differentiators to build messaging around**:
1. Multi-vertical (not just one industry)
2. Focused on commission only (not a bundled CRM)
3. Rep-first real-time dashboard (the "pay anxiety" solver)
4. Clawback automation as first-class feature
5. Simple flat-rate pricing without long-term contracts

**Acquisition channels**:
- LinkedIn: Solar Sales Manager / Director of Sales titles
- Solar industry events (RE+ conference, regional solar meetups)
- Content: "solar sales commission tracking" — low-competition SEO keyword

### 8.3 Phase 3: Roofing Expansion (Months 8–14)

**Target**: Post-storm canvassing companies (these scale instantly after hail/wind events)

**Product requirements for roofing**:
- Insurance contingency commission structure (commission adjusts based on insurance settlement amount)
- Storm event commission bonuses
- Subcontractor (1099) heavy workforce support

**Acquisition channels**:
- NRCA (National Roofing Contractors Association) membership directory
- Roofing Facebook groups ("Roofing Business Owners")
- Direct outreach to largest storm-chasing roofing companies in TX, CO, MN (hail belt)

### 8.4 Lifetime Deal (LTD) Viability Assessment

**Verdict: MRR is the stronger play. LTD has significant structural challenges for this product.**

**Problems with LTD for D2D commission tracking**:
1. **Per-rep billing makes LTD pricing impossible to structure** — A $199 LTD for "up to 10 reps" is undercut immediately by any company that grows beyond 10 reps. LTD buyers then feel entitled to unlimited reps forever.
2. **Seasonality complexity** — Pest control operators add 30 reps in May and drop to 5 in October. An LTD "for 30 reps" is awkward to bill.
3. **AppSumo economics** — AppSumo takes 70–80% of deal revenue. At $199 LTD × 500 buyers = $99,500 gross → $20,000–$30,000 to founder. That's 3–4 months of revenue replacement but creates 500 price-anchored customers who'll resist transitioning to MRR.
4. **Operational burden** — LTD buyers have the highest support expectations and lowest lifetime value per unit of time spent.

**What LTD could work for**: A one-time "Starter Setup" add-on ($299 one-time for white-glove onboarding + commission structure migration) — similar to D2DHQ's model.

**Recommended alternative to LTD**: Annual billing discount (2 months free = 17% off for annual commitment) achieves the retention goal without the LTD pricing trap.

## 9. Risk Assessment and Mitigation

### 9.1 Competitive Risks

**Risk 1 — DASHP expands to solar/roofing**
- Probability: Medium (solo founder with limited engineering capacity)
- Impact: High (eliminates primary differentiation)
- Mitigation: Speed to market; build solar commission templates before DASHP does; build distribution relationships (FB group presence, trade show visibility) that create switching costs

**Risk 2 — Sequifi targets the small-team market with a simplified tier**
- Probability: Low (Sequifi's go-to-market is mid-market enterprise)
- Impact: High (Sequifi has the deepest commission logic)
- Mitigation: Compete on simplicity and rep-first UX — features Sequifi can't easily strip out of their enterprise-first product

**Risk 3 — SalesRabbit or Knockbase deepens commission tracking**
- Probability: Medium (both are actively investing in commission features)
- Impact: Medium (they're CRM-first; commission is still a side feature)
- Mitigation: Position as "commission-only" tool that integrates with their CRM rather than competing with it

### 9.2 Market Risks

**Risk 1 — Seasonal billing volatility**
- D2D rep rosters fluctuate 3–5× from off-season to peak season
- Mitigation: DASHP's flex-license model (month-to-month at 2× rate, capped at 30% of seats) already solved this; adopt the same structure

**Risk 2 — Commission structure complexity**
- Every operator has idiosyncratic commission rules; a rigid calculation engine won't fit
- Mitigation: Build a flexible rules engine from day one (tiered rates, flat rates, percentage, overrides, clawbacks — configurable, not hardcoded)

**Risk 3 — PE roll-up consolidation in pest control**
- As PE platforms consolidate pest control, they may mandate enterprise commission systems
- Mitigation: Enterprise-compatible API / export features; position as a "plug-in" to larger systems rather than fighting them

### 9.3 Execution Risks

**Risk 1 — Commission calculation errors**
- If the software makes payroll errors, it's worse than Excel; reps will blame the software and the manager simultaneously
- Mitigation: Extensive test suite for commission calculation engine; audit trail for every calculation; rep-visible calculation breakdown

**Risk 2 — Customer support burden**
- Commission configurations are highly customized; onboarding will require hands-on support
- Mitigation: White-glove onboarding as a one-time paid service ($299–$500); build self-serve configuration templates per vertical

## Market Research Conclusion

### Summary of Key Findings

1. **Market is real and validated**: DASHP $100K ARR bootstrapped solo in pest control alone proves the exact problem, buyer, and willingness to pay. The "what is your company called and how much does it cost?" first-meeting signal is textbook PMF.

2. **Workforce TAM is substantial**: 112K–225K active D2D sales reps across pest control, solar, home security, and roofing — growing to 250K–400K at seasonal peak. At $20–25/rep/mo, this represents $26M–$120M in addressable ARR for a focused D2D commission tool.

3. **Competitive gap is clear**: No product occupies the multi-vertical, commission-focused, $15–25/rep/mo position. DASHP is single-vertical. Sequifi is full-stack payroll (over-complex for small operators). D2DHQ is deal-based and solar/roofing focused. SalesRabbit/Knockbase are CRM-first with commission as a side feature.

4. **Pain intensity is high**: 60%+ of reps have experienced commission errors; 22% have annual disputes; 9% quit over commission issues. Rep replacement costs $115K–$150K. The ROI case for a $1,000/mo commission tool is trivial to make.

5. **LTD not recommended**: Per-rep billing + seasonal workforce = structural incompatibility with LTD pricing. MRR with annual discount (2 months free) is the stronger play.

6. **Go-to-market is clear**: Pest control first (validated channel, DASHP competitor exists, operators are aware of the category). Solar second (10× rep workforce, higher willingness to pay, more complex commission structures = more value delivered). Roofing third.

### Strategic Market Impact

This is a $3M–$10M ARR opportunity within 24–36 months for a focused operator executing the pest-control-first, multi-vertical-expansion strategy. The retention characteristics (payroll-adjacent, reps revolt against switching) and the operational pain intensity (managers losing Sundays to spreadsheets) create the conditions for a category-defining, high-retention SaaS business in an industry segment that the major commission software vendors have explicitly ignored.
