# Product Brief: GraveKeeper — Cemetery Management SaaS

---

## Executive Summary

GraveKeeper is a cloud-native cemetery management platform targeting the 80,000+ small municipal, church-run, and privately-owned cemeteries in the United States that remain trapped on 1990s desktop software or spreadsheets. The market has a structurally validated willingness to pay (legacy desktop licenses command $2,000–$10,000/year) but lacks a modern, affordable, easy-to-adopt alternative for small operators.

The market has evolved since initial evaluation in August 2026: several affordable SaaS players have emerged (CemeteryBase at $99/mo, Cemify, Chronicle, Everspot). GraveKeeper's positioning is therefore sharpened around two defensible angles: (1) **white-glove managed onboarding** — solving the #1 adoption barrier of data migration — and (2) **church/faith-based vertical specialization** — serving the 30,000–50,000 church-operated cemeteries with parish-specific features no current affordable platform provides.

The business model targets $99–$149/mo recurring SaaS with a parallel $299–$599 Lifetime Deal track for early-stage validation. Total addressable market at 1% penetration of 80,000 small cemeteries yields $950K–$1.9M ARR — a durable, low-churn indie SaaS business with essentially zero VC competition.

## Core Vision

### Problem Statement

130,000 cemeteries operate in the US. The vast majority — 80,000+ small municipal, church-run, and family-owned cemeteries — manage their burial records, plot maps, interment scheduling, and family inquiries using 1990s desktop software (Cemetery Workstation, Sacred Grounds desktop), Excel spreadsheets, or paper ledgers.

The specific workflow gaps creating daily pain:

1. **No interactive plot map** — staff answer hundreds of phone calls from families searching for grave locations because there is no digital map showing which plots are sold, reserved, available, or occupied
2. **Unsearchable burial records** — paper ledgers from 100+ years of burials cannot be searched digitally; family history researchers must call the office or visit in person
3. **Interment scheduling via phone/fax/email** — coordination between cemetery, funeral home, and family for new burials is fragmented across phone calls, fax, and email threads with no structured workflow
4. **Revenue tracking in separate systems** — plot sales, perpetual care fees, and monument permits are tracked in disconnected Excel files and physical receipts
5. **No family self-service portal** — families cannot search for grave locations online without calling the office during business hours

### Problem Impact

The consequences of these workflow gaps are tangible and emotionally high-stakes:

- **Data fragility:** When a long-tenured administrator retires or hardware fails, decades of institutional knowledge and burial records are at risk of being lost permanently
- **Administrative burden:** Cemetery administrators spend 30–50% of their time answering location lookup phone calls that could be self-served
- **Family frustration:** Families searching for ancestors or recently buried loved ones face barriers that feel disrespectful given the emotional context
- **Institutional risk:** Municipal boards and church councils increasingly demand cloud backup and disaster recovery for records that are legally and historically irreplaceable
- **Revenue leakage:** Perpetual care fees go unbilled or uncollected because there is no automated invoicing system

### Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

**Enterprise tier (PlotBox):** Designed for large commercial cemeteries and DSO groups. Custom pricing, complex sales process, not self-serve. Inaccessible to small operators in both price and complexity.

**Mid-market tier (CemeteryBase, Cemify, Chronicle):** The closest competitive zone. CemeteryBase ($99/mo, all-inclusive) and Cemify ($67–158/mo annually) are direct competitors with solid core feature sets. However, all three lack:
- White-glove managed data migration (self-serve only)
- Interment scheduling with funeral home coordination
- Perpetual care fee billing automation
- Church/faith-based vertical features (diocesan reporting, sacramental record linkage)
- State-specific compliance workflows

**Free tier (Everspot, Chronicle Basic):** GPS-enabled mapping and basic records at zero cost. Competes on price but not on service quality, support depth, or institutional feature needs. Municipal and church buyers prioritize reliability and support over cost minimization.

**Legacy desktop (Cemetery Workstation):** The incumbent that GraveKeeper replaces. Desktop-bound, requires Windows installation, no cloud backup, no family portal. Still commanding $2,000–$10,000/year — demonstrating validated willingness to pay that a modern $99–149/mo SaaS easily undercuts on ROI.

### Proposed Solution

GraveKeeper is a cloud-native cemetery management platform with a managed onboarding service model. The core differentiation:

**"We do the hard work for you."**

Rather than competing head-on with CemeteryBase on features at the same price, GraveKeeper leads with a managed migration service: for a one-time fee ($500–$2,000 depending on record volume), GraveKeeper's team digitizes the cemetery's paper records, builds their plot map, and migrates their data from legacy software — then converts to $99–149/mo recurring subscription.

This solves the #1 adoption barrier in the market (migration fear) as a business model differentiator, not just a feature. No affordable competitor does this.

The platform covers:
- Interactive SVG/Canvas plot map with real-time status
- Searchable burial records database with CSV/Excel import
- Interment scheduling with funeral home coordination workflow
- Public-facing family search portal (embeddable on cemetery website)
- Plot sales, deed generation, perpetual care fee tracking
- Church/faith-based specific features (Phase 2)

### Key Differentiators

1. **Managed onboarding as a product** — $500–$2,000 one-time migration fee that does the hard work for the cemetery. Competitors offer self-serve only. This is the sales conversation: "We handle the entire migration so you can focus on operations."

2. **Church/faith-based vertical focus** — Dedicated features for the 30,000–50,000 parish cemeteries: diocesan reporting templates, sacramental record field support, multi-language interface for immigrant parish communities, and integration pathways to parish management systems (Planning Center, Church360). No current affordable platform targets this niche.

3. **Interment scheduling workflow** — A structured multi-party coordination module connecting cemetery, funeral home, and family for upcoming burials. No affordable competitor fully addresses this process gap; it's currently done via phone and email threads.

4. **Perpetual care fee automation** — Automated annual invoicing and ACH collection for endowed care agreements. Completely unaddressed at the affordable price tier.

5. **Institutional trust positioning** — White-glove service model, data security emphasis, and explicit partnership with ICCFA/state cemetery associations creates the trust signals that institutional buyers (municipal, church, diocesan) require.

## Target Users

### Primary Users

#### Persona 1: Margaret — The Overwhelmed Municipal Administrator

**Background:** Margaret, 58, is the Parks & Recreation administrative coordinator for a mid-sized city (pop. ~35,000) in the Midwest. The city cemetery is one of many responsibilities on her plate alongside park permits and recreation program registrations. She has managed cemetery records for 15 years, taking over from a predecessor who worked from paper ledgers.

**Current State:**
- Uses an Excel spreadsheet for plot availability and a separate paper ledger for burial records
- Receives 5–10 phone calls per week from families asking for grave locations, most of which require manual file lookups
- Just learned that her predecessor kept a personal notebook with 300+ records that were never entered into any system
- City council has directed her to "get the records into the cloud" after a water heater leaked near the records room

**Pain Points:**
- Phone call interruptions dominate her morning; she's told her supervisor she needs help but the city won't hire additional staff
- Dreads the thought of migrating 40 years of paper records herself
- Worries about getting something wrong — burial records feel too important to risk a mistake

**What Would Make Her Say "This Is Exactly What I Needed":**
- "They came and helped us get our records in. I didn't have to do it myself."
- Families can look up grave locations online without calling her
- Her city council can see the data is backed up in the cloud

**Decision Authority:** Can initiate the purchase inquiry; city council or city manager approves. Below $5,000/year budget threshold = no formal bidding required. Responds heavily to peer references from other municipal administrators.

#### Persona 2: Father Kevin's Parish Administrator — The Church Secretary Volunteer

**Background:** Linda, 64, is a retired schoolteacher who volunteers as the administrative assistant for St. Agnes Catholic Parish. The parish cemetery is 150 years old with approximately 2,800 plots. The current cemetery records are in a handwritten ledger and a partial Excel file started 12 years ago by a previous administrator. Linda manages cemetery requests as a small fraction of her broader parish duties.

**Current State:**
- Receives 3–5 calls per week for grave location or burial record requests
- The parish priest has asked her to find a way for families to search records online
- The diocese has sent a circular asking parishes to ensure burial records have a digital backup
- She found a cloud option online (CemeteryBase) but was intimidated by the self-setup requirement and concerned about importing 150 years of records herself

**Pain Points:**
- She has no IT support; the parish can't afford a consultant to help set things up
- The diocesan reporting request adds pressure: she needs to submit cemetery statistics annually
- Wants to honor the families represented in the records — making a mistake feels deeply inappropriate in this context

**What Would Make Her Say "This Is Exactly What I Needed":**
- "Someone walked me through the whole setup and imported our old records."
- Has a public portal she can link to from the parish website for families to search
- Has a simple report she can submit to the diocese annually

**Decision Authority:** Recommends to the parish priest and finance committee. Budget usually approved informally under $2,000/year. Parish association network (diocesan newsletter, Catholic Cemetery Association) is the most trusted referral source.

#### Persona 3: Dave — The Private Cemetery Owner-Operator

**Background:** Dave, 47, owns and operates a family-owned cemetery in a rural county. He inherited the business from his father and manages it himself with one part-time groundskeeper. The cemetery has ~1,200 plots and runs Cemetery Workstation on an aging Dell desktop. He's been putting off upgrading because "it works, mostly" but had a scare last year when the hard drive failed and he nearly lost all the records.

**Current State:**
- Paying $2,400/year for Cemetery Workstation — knows he's overpaying for the support contract on outdated software
- Hard drive scare motivated him to start searching for cloud alternatives
- Compared CemeteryBase ($99/mo), Cemify ($799/yr), and one other option; got confused by pricing differences
- Key concern: "I need to be able to get my data out if I don't like it"

**Pain Points:**
- Doesn't want to rebuild his plot map from scratch; the data migration work feels enormous
- Suspicious of SaaS pricing ("you're paying forever and never own it")
- Wants a clear ROI story: currently spending $2,400/year on desktop software, wants to understand how much this will cost long-term

**What Would Make Him Say "This Is Exactly What I Needed":**
- "They migrated my Cemetery Workstation data for me — I didn't have to do anything"
- LTD option or clear annual pricing with data export guarantee
- Lower annual cost than his current $2,400/year desktop license

**Decision Authority:** Sole decision-maker. Price-sensitive but spending $2,400/year on desktop = motivated to find savings. Responds well to ROI framing. Finds vendors through Google search and comparison sites (Capterra, GetApp).

### Secondary Users

**Family Members (Genealogy Researchers and Recent Bereaved):** Not paying customers but heavy users of the public family search portal. Their experience drives word-of-mouth value and reduces the cemetery administrator's phone call burden. They access GraveKeeper only through the embeddable public portal on the cemetery's website.

**Funeral Home Directors:** Interact with the interment scheduling module to coordinate upcoming burials. They are not direct customers but are key workflow participants. Their adoption of the scheduling coordination workflow determines its success.

**Cemetery Groundskeeping Crew:** Field staff who need mobile access to plot maps and work orders. Secondary users of the mobile interface for verifying plot locations during interment or maintenance.

**Diocesan / Denominational Administrators (Church vertical):** Receive annual reports and compliance submissions. Interact with GraveKeeper indirectly through report exports from parish administrators.

## MVP Scope

### Core Features (Sprint 1 — 4–6 Weeks)

**1. Interactive Plot Map**
- SVG/Canvas-based visual cemetery map with drag-to-zoom and click-to-select
- Plot status visualization: Available (green), Reserved (yellow), Sold/Not Yet Buried (orange), Buried (grey), Unavailable (red)
- Click on plot: shows owner name, burial date, plot number, section/row/lot identifier
- Admin can update plot status from the map view
- CSV/Excel import for plot data (row, section, lot, status, owner, dates)

**2. Burial Records Database**
- Searchable full-text search across all burial records (name, date, plot number, section)
- Record fields: deceased name, dates (birth/death/burial), plot reference, officiating minister/funeral home, family contact, notes
- CSV/Excel import for migrating existing records
- Basic record export (CSV, PDF)
- Link record to plot on map

**3. Interment Scheduling Module**
- Create upcoming interment record: deceased info, date/time, funeral home contact, family contact, gravedigger notes
- Status workflow: Requested → Confirmed → Completed
- Email notification to funeral home contact on confirmation
- Calendar view of upcoming interments
- Notes field for coordination messages

**4. Plot Sales & Basic Revenue Tracking**
- Record plot purchase: buyer, date, amount paid, payment method
- Generate deed PDF (from customizable template)
- Basic revenue log: list of plot sales with dates and amounts
- Mark plots as sold/reserved from sales record

**5. Family Search Portal**
- Embeddable iframe/widget for the cemetery's website
- Search by deceased name, date range, section/plot number
- Display results: name, dates, plot location (section/row/lot), optional photo of grave marker
- No login required for families; read-only access to public records only

**6. Admin Dashboard**
- Summary: total plots, plots sold, plots available, upcoming interments this month
- Recent activity feed
- Quick search across all records
- User management (admin + limited staff role)

**7. Onboarding Flow**
- Guided setup wizard: cemetery name, location, plot numbering scheme, import data
- Migration service booking option (CTA to schedule a call for managed migration)
- Sample data sandbox to test before importing live records

### Out of Scope for MVP

The following are confirmed Phase 2 items and will not be in MVP:

- **Perpetual care fee billing and ACH collection** — complex financial workflows; Phase 2
- **Church/faith-based specific features** (diocesan reporting, sacramental record fields, multi-language) — requires domain validation with church customers first; Phase 2
- **QuickBooks / accounting integration** — Phase 2
- **Mobile groundskeeping app** — web-responsive design is sufficient for MVP; native app is Phase 3
- **GPS grave navigation** (smartphone walk-to-grave navigation) — Phase 2
- **Historical record OCR/AI digitization** — managed service addresses this manually for MVP
- **Multi-site / cemetery group management** — single-cemetery only for MVP
- **Monument permit management** — Phase 2
- **FamilySearch / Ancestry.com API integration** — Phase 2
- **Pre-need trust management** — too complex for MVP

### MVP Success Criteria

The MVP is validated and ready for full investment when:

1. **10 paying customers** at $99+/month within 90 days of launch
2. **5 managed migration engagements** completed with verifiable satisfaction (written testimonials)
3. **Public portal activation** for at least 7 of the 10 paying customers (portal live on their website)
4. **Net Promoter Score > 50** in first-customer survey (cemetery administrators recommending to peers)
5. **Churn: 0 cancellations** in first 90 days (data lock-in should prevent early churn; any cancellation is a serious signal)
6. **Channel signal:** At least 1 inbound inquiry from an association/referral source (validates word-of-mouth channel)

These criteria together validate the core hypothesis: that managed migration + core features creates enough value for cemetery administrators to pay and stay.
