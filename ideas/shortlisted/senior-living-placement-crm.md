## One-Line Pitch
The CRM for senior placement coordinators — manage families through the placement process, track facility options, and auto-calculate commissions, all in one tool they won't outgrow in a week.

## Problem

Senior living placement coordinators (who earn $2,000–$5,000 per successful placement) have no dedicated vertical software. They manage their entire workflow — tracking families through emotional, multi-week placement journeys — in Excel spreadsheets or generic CRMs like HubSpot that know nothing about senior living facilities, care levels, or commission structures.

The pain is real and the market is specific:
- Each placement involves: multiple facilities, family members with conflicting preferences, care needs assessment, insurance/funding eligibility, tours, negotiations, and commission tracking
- A coordinator typically manages 20–40 active families at once, each at a different stage
- Commission is paid by the facility upon move-in and is ~1–2 months of the resident's monthly rent — tracking which placement completed, which is pending, and when commission will arrive is a full accounting challenge in Excel
- Generic CRMs (HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive) have no concept of "facility database", "care level", "Medicare/Medicaid eligibility", or "family team"

**The IH signal**: An Indie Hackers discussion specifically names dental management software reaching $9,747 MRR as a "super boring SaaS" — the same pattern applies exactly to senior living placement. Both are unglamorous professional services where the specialist buys vertical tools, not the end consumer.

## Market Evidence

- ~30,000 senior living placement agencies in the US (estimate from industry data)
- Agencies range from solo coordinators to regional networks (A Place for Mom is the large marketplace; placement coordinator tools serve the independent agent layer)
- Senior living market growing: aging US demographics mean placement volumes are increasing through 2035
- "A Place for Mom" and "Caring.com" are marketplaces — they don't solve the coordinator's workflow problem
- IH reference: dental management SaaS at $9,747 MRR = directly analogous boring-SaaS playbook
- No known indie vertical CRM for this segment confirmed (generic CRM + Excel is the current stack)

## Product Concept: "PlaceDesk"

**Pricing**: $79/mo (solo coordinator) / $149/mo (small team, up to 5 users)
**LTD**: $149 (solo) / $299 (team) on AppSumo

**Core MVP (3–4 weeks):**
1. **Family pipeline** — Kanban-style pipeline stages: Inquiry → Assessment → Touring → Pending Move-In → Placed → Commission Pending → Commission Received
2. **Family profiles** — family members, contact info, care needs (memory care, assisted living, independent), budget range, preferred location, timeline
3. **Facility database** — searchable facility list with care levels offered, price ranges, availability notes, contact info, commission rates
4. **Communication log** — every email/call/text automatically time-stamped per family; template library for common messages (first contact, tour confirmation, follow-up)
5. **Commission tracker** — auto-calculates expected commission based on move-in date and facility rate; tracks pending vs. received; monthly commission forecast view
6. **Automated follow-up reminders** — "Family hasn't been contacted in 7 days" alerts; post-move-in check-in reminders at 30/60/90 days

**Phase 2:**
- AI-drafted family assessment notes from intake call
- Facility matching score (family needs vs. facility capabilities)
- Family portal (families can see their placement journey, submit documents)
- Integration with DocuSign for authorization letters

## Key Differentiators
1. **Placement-specific pipeline stages** — not generic sales stages; built around the actual placement journey
2. **Commission calculator + forecast** — tracks when commission will arrive based on move-in date; unique to this niche
3. **Facility database** — pre-populated with searchable facilities (or coordinator-maintained); generic CRMs have none of this
4. **Family team support** — one placement often involves 3–5 family decision-makers; built-in family group management
