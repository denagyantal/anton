# HN & Indie Hackers Research — 2026-09-15

## Summary

Today's research surfaced a consistent and strong signal: boring, unsexy, trade and field-service industries remain deeply underserved by software, and both YC and the indie hacker community are actively pointing at them as the highest-opportunity space for 2026. Key themes:

1. **The "serviceable" gap is real and growing** — 89% of tradespeople reportedly lack adequate digital tools (SBA Q3 2025 Technology Adoption Report). Existing solutions (ServiceTitan at $245-500/tech/mo) are priced for enterprise; the SMB/0–10 employee shop is wide open.
2. **YC is explicitly calling for boring-industry AI tools** — Spring 2026 RFS named "AI-Guided Physical Work" as a key category. HVAC repair assistant, field inspection tools, and trade-specific AI copilots are flagged as high-fit for solo founders.
3. **Validated products are shipping in these niches** — FieldLedgr (field service), SuperCMMS (maintenance/work orders), YardBook/Yardbook YC W16 (landscaping), Latchel YC W19 (property maintenance), Kanda YC W21 (tradesperson financing), and Rudus YC P26 (concrete estimation) are all real products in boring niches showing HN engagement and, in several cases, paying customers.
4. **The "no-bullshit, cheap, small team" angle is explicitly unserved** — A founder building Documentorium noted "hundreds of paying users" for a dead-simple document/PDF engine for trades — validating ultra-simple tools with flat pricing.
5. **Software margins in boring B2B** — Multiple sources confirm 68% of profitable solo SaaS businesses serve a vertical niche. Median profitable micro-SaaS sits at ~$4,200 MRR. Industrial/trade software has notoriously low competition and high willingness to pay.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

### Industries with highest signal today:
1. **HVAC / Trades** — massive TAM (118K HVAC contractors), specific pain points (maintenance agreements, estimation, scheduling), existing software is either too expensive or too old
2. **Property Management** — API fragmentation creates layer opportunities; maintenance coordination is a service gap not a software gap
3. **Small Fleet (5-20 vehicles)** — service businesses with vans are underserved by both consumer and enterprise fleet tools
4. **Construction Estimation** — Rudus proved the model for concrete; same approach applies to roofing, electrical, drywall

### What works for LTDs (AppSumo-ready ideas):
- Simple, focused tools with clear daily utility (estimate generator, maintenance agreement tracker)
- "Alternative to [expensive incumbent]" positioning
- Flat pricing (no per-user), no complex setup
- Top candidates: #8 Documentorium-model estimate tool, #10 HVAC Maintenance Agreement Manager, #7 Fleet Management for small operators

### What HN signals but underconfirms:
- Concrete/construction AI is validated by YC backing but comments express skepticism about industry knowledge depth — suggests domain expertise is a real moat
- Property management software is crowded at the PM level; opportunity is in specific pain points (maintenance coordination, payment reminders) not general platforms
