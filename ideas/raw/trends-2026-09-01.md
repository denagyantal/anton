## Top 3 White-Space Opportunities

### Opportunity 1: Lightweight SMS/WhatsApp Maintenance Bot for Small Landlords
**Why now**: Property management AI is booming (CentralComs, Brickwise, EliseAI) but all target 500+ unit operators. The 15M+ US individual landlords managing 1-10 units coordinate maintenance via text and phone — there is zero software serving this segment at a price they'll pay ($19-49/mo). An SMS-first chatbot that tenants text with maintenance requests, AI triages urgency, and the landlord approves a pre-vetted vendor with one reply would have near-zero churn and very low support burden. MVP in 3-4 weeks. LTD at $149 would convert well on AppSumo and real estate investor communities.

### Opportunity 2: EPA Pesticide Application Compliance Log for Lawn/Pest Control
**Why now**: 500,000+ licensed lawn and pest control applicators in the US are legally required to maintain detailed chemical application records. Most use paper or Excel. RealGreen/FieldRoutes (the only software options with compliance built in) cost $200-400/mo — well out of reach for a 1-3 truck solo operation. A focused $29-49/mo (or $149 LTD) mobile app for chemical logging, with state-specific report generation and license expiration reminders, would face no direct competition in this price range. Clear regulatory ROI makes the sales pitch easy.

### Opportunity 3: AI Photo-to-Quote Tool for Residential Tradespeople
**Why now**: XBuild ($19M raised), Rudus (YC S26), and DeepLawn prove that AI estimation is a validated market. But all of these tools require uploaded PDFs or satellite data — too complex for the plumber quoting a bathroom remodel or the painter quoting a living room. A smartphone camera → natural language description → itemized line-item quote workflow is missing. A $49-79/mo (or $199-299 LTD) tool where a tradesperson takes 3 photos and speaks a 30-second description, and gets a formatted PDF quote in 90 seconds, would serve millions of solo operators who currently write quotes on paper or take 30 minutes in Excel.
