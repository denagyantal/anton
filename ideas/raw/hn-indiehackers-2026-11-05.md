- **Core Value Prop**: DOT compliance SaaS that tracks driver qualification files (49 CFR §391.51), drug & alcohol programs (§382 random pool + Clearinghouse), DVIR/inspection/maintenance records (§396), and auto-enforces document retention periods. - **Our Angle**: If Roadworthy HQ stalls (no revenue at 6 months post-launch), there's a clear opportunity to build a competing product targeting just 2–3 of the most painful compliance documents (DQ file + D&A program) with a freemium tier that captures the market before charging. Alternatively: build the "fleet inspection + DVIR" mobile app as a standalone product priced per-driver/month.
- **LTD Potential**: 4/5 — compliance tools have strong willingness to pay; fear of FMCSA fines is a powerful motivator; LTD could work for fleet owner-operators.

## Trends & Patterns

### 1. The Boring Vertical SaaS Moment Has Arrived
2026 is unambiguously the year that "boring" vertical SaaS went mainstream in the indie hacker + HN community. Multiple posts explicitly named unglamorous industries (septic, pest control, trucking compliance, maid services) as the best opportunities — and backed it up with real revenue numbers. The market consensus: VC ignores these markets, so competition is low, willingness to pay is high, and switching costs build naturally.

### 2. Revenue Validation Across Multiple Niches
Hard revenue data found today:
- ZenMaid (maid services): $250K/month MRR = $3M/yr
- APMHelp (property management accounting): $7M ARR
- Septic service SaaS (unnamed): $40K MRR in 9 months
- Boring local classifieds + directory: $25K+ MRR after 5 years
- Mobile notary SaaS: ~$49K MRR at $45/month × 1,100+ subscribers

### 3. AI Is Coming to Boring Industries — Not Here Yet
The clearest pattern: AI automation is being applied to boring B2B sectors in 2025–2026, but most products are early-stage or pre-revenue. The HN Show HN about "Autonomous Home Services Business" (running 3 live cleaning companies) is the most extreme example. AI dental admin (Denta), AI property management agents (Domos, PM-in-a-Box), AI back-office for HVAC (Faraday) all launched in this window. The winners will be whoever combines narrow industry focus with reliable AI execution — not the most technically sophisticated AI.

### 4. The 1–10 Employee Segment Is Wide Open
Every major vertical SaaS player (ServiceTitan, SingleOps, WorkWave) prices for businesses with 10+ employees and $1M+ revenue. The 1–10 employee segment of every trade (HVAC, pest control, landscaping, cleaning) is served by nothing modern. These businesses have $500K–$2M revenue and would pay $79–$199/month for good software — but are priced out of enterprise tools and ignored by startups chasing larger contracts.

### 5. Compliance Is a Forcing Function for SaaS Adoption
DOT compliance (Roadworthy HQ), pest control equipment compliance, dental HIPAA compliance, property management trust account compliance — regulated industries must keep records. Compliance SaaS sells itself because the alternative is an audit or fine. Products built around compliance deadlines have natural urgency and near-zero churn.

### 6. Local SEO + Trade Association Distribution Beats Paid Ads
Multiple founders explicitly noted that paid social failed and local SEO + community outreach worked. For boring B2B verticals: pest control associations, HVAC trade groups, property management association (NARPM), landscaping industry forums, and state trucking associations are the highest-ROI distribution channels. Products that can partner with or be recommended by industry associations (AppFolio's partner marketplace, Buildium's partner network) unlock immediate access to concentrated audiences.

### 7. The "Boring" Niche Stack
The highest-signal niches in order of immediacy (how fast a bootstrapped founder could reach $5K–$20K MRR):
