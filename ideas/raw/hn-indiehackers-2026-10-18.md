## Top Themes & Patterns

1. **The $49–$199/month gap is real and getting wider.** ServiceTitan ($770M+ funding), BuildOps ($127M, $1B valuation), and Probook ($40M) are all pushing upmarket. This leaves solo operators and micro-businesses (1–10 employees) chronically underserved at the $19–79/month price point.

2. **AI is being layered on top of boring businesses, not replacing them.** The most fundable companies (Probook, Rainslice, Zirco) are using AI to automate the repetitive 80% of back-office work (calls, quotes, scheduling, follow-up) in home services and medical offices. This pattern is repeatable for any service business with predictable workflows.

3. **Compliance is an underappreciated recurring pain.** Permit tracking, license renewal, EPA chemical compliance, insurance certificate management — every regulated service business has this problem and it's currently a spreadsheet. A simple $29/month tracker has near-zero churn because the cost of missing a renewal is catastrophic.

4. **Hyper-vertical beats horizontal.** Housecall Pro just launched "trade-specific" packages in July 2026 — confirming the market wants tools built for one trade, not generic field service software. The opportunity is to go even more specific: pest-control-only, septic-only, junk-removal-only.

5. **SEO moats in boring niches are durable.** The bank statement converter ($10K MRR, pure SEO), the local classifieds site ($25K MRR, hyperlocal SEO), and the mobile notary platform ($49K MRR, niche SEO) all built recurring revenue without paid acquisition by owning long-tail search queries nobody else is targeting.

6. **Market acquisition exits validate the space.** PestRoutes acquired for $300M (pest control software). BuildOps unicorn at $1B. ServiceTitan acquired Conduit Tech. These exits confirm that boring vertical SaaS is highly acquirable at strong multiples, making it attractive for LTD-first bootstrappers who want a credible exit path.
